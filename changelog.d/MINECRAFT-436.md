### Changed
- MINECRAFT-436: per-player atmosphere/smoke/material network sync is now paced under an explicit, configurable per-player operation budget (`runtime.maxSyncOperationsPerPlayerPerTick`, default 4096 chunk/cell operations) instead of sending an entire full-resync snapshot's batches in the single tick that generated them. A snapshot too large to fit the budget in one tick is now delivered as a bounded, fair continuation across several ticks: a player is guaranteed at least one queued batch of progress per tick (a single oversized batch can never starve them), and a newer full snapshot (dimension change, view-distance change, or the next periodic full resync) always supersedes any still-undelivered batches of an earlier, incomplete one, so a client never receives a mix of two generations' worth of data. Ordinary chunk-crossing deltas remain delta-based and unaffected when no snapshot is in flight. Periodic full resyncs (`runtime.fullSnapshotIntervalTicks`) are now also staggered per player (a stable hash of the player's UUID picks their phase within the interval) instead of every connected player's full re-snapshot landing on the exact same tick. No wire/packet format change: `AtmosphereGridPayload`/`SmokeGridPayload`/`MaterialGridPayload` and their `reset`/`snapshotEnd` semantics are unchanged, only the tick-by-tick pacing of when already-planned batches are sent.

### Added
- `runtime.maxSyncOperationsPerPlayerPerTick` server config option (default `4096`, range `1`-`1000000`): the per-player, per-sync-channel (main atmosphere, smoke, and each material) cap on chunk/cell operations released from the paced send queue each sync tick.
- `PacedSyncQueue`, with unit coverage for budget enforcement, guaranteed per-tick progress on an oversized batch, reset-supersedes-stale-queue behavior, delta-after-snapshot ordering, and disconnect/retain/clear release of queued work.
- Regression coverage proving per-player periodic full-snapshot staggering: each player is due for exactly one full resync per configured interval, distinct players' due ticks spread across the interval rather than coinciding, and a given player's due tick is a stable, deterministic function of their UUID and the interval.
